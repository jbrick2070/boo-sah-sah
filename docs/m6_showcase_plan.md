# BOO SAH SAH on M6

**Build a 30-second native Mac preview first.** The Shenzhen flyover at 4:05–4:35 puts the six-rotor aircraft, distant skyline and intercepted video in the same shot. It also gives us a reference frame at 4:20 to match against Windows. M6 has a Metal GPU with Neural Accelerators, a separate dual 16-core Neural Engine, and hardware H.264 encode and decode. This proposal targets Jeffrey’s M6. Performance on that machine is still unmeasured. [Apple’s M6 announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-introduces-m6-and-m5-ultra-for-a-big-leap-in-performance-and-ai-compute/) · [M6 hardware specifications](https://www.apple.com/mac-mini/specs/)

## First playable scene

Reuse the existing Shenzhen aircraft mesh, skyline layout, camera path, cue timing, soundtrack FLACs and string catalogs. Start the flyover from the same audio sample on each run. Port the city materials, aircraft lighting and native-resolution composite to Metal. At 4:20, check the cockpit silhouette, rotor edges and farthest visible buildings against the Windows capture. Then add the 720p Signal Hijack inset while the main scene continues at its chosen resolution. [Aircraft and city reference](graphics_update_plan.md)

## What the comparison should reveal

For correctness, replay identical feature vectors through the CPU and ANE configurations. Compare all ten outputs, report their maximum absolute difference, and compare warm latency at the same prediction cadence. Near-identical pictures are a successful result. For the artistic comparison, freeze the same camera and audio cue and let viewers switch between **Madame N** and the **scripted director**. That comparison shows what the model contributes to bloom, iris and lighting. A full Mac port is worth pursuing if the flyover reads well in motion and the on-device trace shows which hardware did the work.
